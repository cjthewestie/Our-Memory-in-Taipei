# Our Memory in Taipei

> **▶ Open it: https://cjthewestie.github.io/Our-Memory-in-Taipei/**
> Walk it freely, or take the guided tour.

Started at Claude Code Build Day, 2026-09-20, and still being built.

We wanted this to be a page that could carry everyone's memory of Taipei. From the ten dollars our mothers gave us in Ximending when we were small, all the way to the day we could finally build something of our own — and we look up at Taipei 101, and the man high on the tower is waving at us.

**Let's climb higher, together.**

So every building here has a real one behind it: the eight blocks of 中華商場, the hand-painted billboards of 樂聲戲院, the octagon of the Red House, the three gable styles of Dihua Street, the swallowtail ridge of 霞海城隍廟, the red sign of 永樂市場. Not a backdrop — a street you may have actually walked down.

You arrive in a square at golden hour, under a great banyan wrapped in red cloth with a small 土地公 shrine at its foot: uncles at 象棋 and aunties over 老人茶 beneath it, arcades all round with shop signs from every decade, a sausage cart, a little open-air karaoke stand (walk up and press `E` to start a song), and a notice board where everyone's newest memories are pinned up (press `E` there to read them all). Three gates stand where their districts are in the real city: 西門町 to the **west** (西門 is literally "the west gate"), 大稻埕 to the **north**, 信義 to the **east**. Walk through one and you are in that part of Taipei, in its decade, steering the girl in red wherever you like. An arch at each end of a section leads back to the square or on to the next one, and 信義 ends at the foot of Taipei 101, where you can look up at the man climbing it.

**Guided tour** plays the original film: scrolling moves you down one street and forward through forty years, with the big words surfacing as you go, ending on the man climbing Taipei 101. Open it with the button, or with `?tour=1` (`?year=1993` jumps straight to a year).

## Your memories

Anyone can leave a memory where it happened. Press **+ Leave a memory** wherever you are standing: the form (Chinese first, English under it) offers a fill-in line for that place, such as 「你第一次自己去西門町，是去……」 in Ximending or 「你在 101 下面等誰？」 in Xinyi, or 「或者，寫你想寫的」 for anything else; asks whose memory it is (我的 mine, 爸媽的 my parents', 阿公阿嬤的 my grandparents': ask them at home); and offers only the years that street is (Xinyi cannot be 1962; the square goes back to 1950). It can have a one-line title and name its place, one of the street's landmarks (紅樓前, 武昌街電影街, 霞海城隍廟口, 迪化街騎樓下, 101 腳下……); left as it is, the nearest one is named, and choosing another hangs the lantern there instead. A name and a photo are optional. It hangs there as a paper lantern for the next visitor to find (walk up and the bubble shows its year), or, in the square, is pinned to the memory board, as the form says. The moment it is sent, you get its link (`?m=<id>`): until the makers have read it, the link says 「還在等作者讀過」; after, it walks whoever opens it to your lantern and opens it.

Every memory reads like a museum label: its title, its place and year, the story, and whose it is (「轉述爸媽的記憶」 for a parent's). Each card has **帶我去那裡 · Take me there**, which walks you to the lantern, and **我也記得 · I remember this too**: press it once and the lantern glows a little warmer. No number is ever shown. The memory board in the square is the whole exhibition: once there are enough memories it has tabs by era and a filter by place, and 「我家在…… · I live near…」 lists the memories nearest a place you choose and takes you there. The photograph panel of a real building lists the memories left near it, oldest first. At most thirty lanterns are lit in a section at once; past that they take turns. Every memory is read by the makers before it appears, and the form says so before you send; its privacy note says what is kept, where, that a photo is re-encoded in your browser so its EXIF location never leaves your phone, and how to have a memory taken down. When the makers pause submissions the form says 「暫停收件」 and the lanterns stay.

The street already holds nine of the makers' own memories (marked 本頁作者 · the makers of this page): 1962 and 1987 in the square, 1990, 1992 and 1994 in Ximending, 2008 and 2014 in Dadaocheng, 2020 and this year in Xinyi. Setup and moderation: [`docs/MEMORIES.md`](docs/MEMORIES.md).

## The photographs

Eight of the buildings carry a marker you can click, and it opens a real photograph of the real place — 中華商場 on its opening day in 1961, Taipei 101 with the cranes still on its crown. At each change of era one photograph passes across the street as you scroll, faint and brief.

Every photograph is licensed, and every one is credited on the page. Some carry share-alike terms; the credits panel names each photographer, holder and licence.

## Sound

Two pieces of music for each decade and two for the square at golden hour, all public domain (from FreePD), and all the page's own files — nothing streams from anywhere else. The player sits bottom-left: play and pause, a bar you can drag, the decade buttons, and the sound switch. On a phone (held either way up) the sound always starts off, and only the sound switch turns it on.

With the sound on you also hear the place itself: cicadas in the banyan, chess pieces and tea cups, the shrine's bell, the sausage cart, pigeons and the karaoke stand's little tune in the square; a crowd in 西門町, a New Year market with firecrackers a few streets away in 大稻埕, the metro passing under 信義, and now and then the garbage truck's 給愛麗絲 a block away; and her footsteps on brick, terrazzo and asphalt. Every track and recording is listed in the Credits panel and in [`asset/music/CREDITS.md`](asset/music/CREDITS.md). On an iPhone with an older Safari the ringer switch can silence the street sounds while the music plays; the page says so once.
