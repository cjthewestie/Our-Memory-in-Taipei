## Modelling notes

"Photo N" below means the number printed under the picture in `reference-sheet.jpg`, not the table row number.

Labels: **measured** = a published figure or a number read off a source image with a stated scale; **inferred** = my reading of the photos; **guess** = not backed by anything here, check before relying on it. Published figures come from the English Wikipedia article "Taipei 101" (fetched 2026-11-01) and the labels on `101-scheme-segments-heights.jpg`.

### Taipei 101 (16 photos)

- Total height to spire tip 508.2 m; roof 449.2 m; top occupied floor 439.2 m. **measured** (Wikipedia, CTBUH figures). So the mast/spire above the roof is about 59 m. **measured** (508.2 - 449.2).
- 101 floors above ground, 5 basements; tower is eight segments of eight floors each. **measured** (Wikipedia).
- Scheme diagram marks 390 m at the top of the eighth segment (crown base), 449 m roof, 509 m spire, 50 m wide at the base. **measured** from the diagram labels; these are the diagram author's annotations, not an official drawing.
- Segment height about 33-34 m each (8 floors x about 4.2 m). **inferred** from the equal pitch of the segment steps in the scheme image (about 112 px against 3.3 px/m) and the usual floor height. So the eight segments run from about 121 m to 390 m, and the pedestal below them from the podium roof to about 121 m. **inferred**
- Podium mall: about 5 retail levels above ground, roof about 25-30 m. **guess**. Photos 12 and 16 show five or six stepped terraces, no number is published here.
- Pedestal (floors 1-25 or so): a square tapered base, 50 m wide at the ground and narrowing upward; side width near the top is about 40 m. **inferred** (50 m label, scheme taper, about 20 percent narrowing across the base).
- Each segment flares outward as it rises (top wider than bottom, by about 3-4 m in total per side-to-side width, about 8 percent). **inferred** from the scheme silhouette. The upper corners are notched; photos 1, 5, 6 show the notch at every corner.
- Coin ornaments: four discs, one on each face, where the pedestal meets the first segment. **measured** (Wikipedia). Diameter about 10 m. **guess** (the diagram shows the disc at roughly a third of face width, not trustworthy under perspective).
- Ruyi ornaments: at least 8 m tall each. **measured** (Wikipedia). Placed at the segment waistlines; photos 7 and 13 show the small ruyi medallion centred on each face at every segment join. **inferred**
- Crown (above 390 m): narrower, stepped, with the observatory level and a rectangular mechanical box on top; then the mast and spire. **inferred** (scheme image).
- Roof/curtain wall: green-tinted glass (the "bamboo" look) in a regular grid, metal bands at every eighth floor where outrigger trusses sit. **measured** (Wikipedia: outriggers at eight-floor intervals, green-tinted glass). Colours: glass teal-green, frames silver-grey, ornaments bronze/gold-grey. **inferred**
- Existing stylised model (`tower101.py`): 129 units total, segment half-width 5.2 to 6.6, pedestal half-width 8.5 to 6.0. Its proportions (pedestal about 1/4, eight segments about 2/3, crown plus spire the rest) match the real order, but nothing there is in metres. **measured** (read from the file header).

### Shin Kong Mitsukoshi Xinyi Place A11 (7 photos)

No published dimensions were found in the sources checked (English Wikipedia, zh Wikipedia, Commons descriptions). Everything below is read off the photos.

- Form: a boxy block clad in brown stone tile, with an open steel frame on the plaza front (raked braces, balconies with railings), a wide overhanging dark roof slab and a planted roof edge. **inferred** from photos 17, 18, 20.
- Rounded corner: no photo here shows a rounded corner. The current scene code uses a rounded corner of radius 6 (`scene-tower.js`, `A11.r`); I could not support it. Treat as **unverified**; check a plan or satellite view before modelling it.
- Height: about 6-7 retail levels above grade in photo 17 (steel frame with tiled upper bands), roughly 30-35 m with floor-to-floor about 5 m. **inferred**. The scene code assumes 12 floors and 34 m; the 12-floor count is **unverified**, the 34 m is close to my reading.
- Footprint: a large block of roughly 80 x 60 m. **guess**.
- Facade grid: tile panels in horizontal bands, about 1.2-1.5 m high (photo 17), no fine grid except on the glass wing at the side; wide bands of dark louvers near the roof. **inferred**
- Entrance: a double-height open front where the steel frame stands on slender dark columns, with a thin glass-and-steel canopy over the entrance (photo 22). **inferred**
- Sign: vertical red pylon with white 新光三越, about 10 m tall at the entrance, with an "A11" plate at the base (photos 17, 20, 21). **inferred**. Colour vermilion red, about #D0221A. **inferred** (read by eye).
- Colours: tile brown-pink (#8A6A5C range) on the 2008 tiled front; dark grey granite and louvers on the street side (photo 22); dark charcoal steel frame. **inferred**
- LED walls: not visible in any of these photos; large printed ad boards on the plaza side (photos 17, 18). **inferred**

### Xinyi skywalk system (12 photos; photo 29 is a different, older bridge kept for contrast)

- Form: elevated covered walkways at second-floor level linking Taipei 101, the A-series malls (A4, A8, A9, A11), Vieshow and the City Hall MRT area, with glass guard sides and a suspended canopy lit by LEDs. **measured** (zh Wikipedia, 信義商圈空橋系統: glass fences, suspended roof, LED lighting). Network layout: `skywalk-system-map.png`.
- Deck width about 4.5-5 m between the glass sides. **inferred** (photo 26: four or five people side by side, shoulder width about 0.5 m). **guess** for the precise value.
- Deck height above the road about 5.5-6.5 m. **inferred** (photo 30 shows the deck well above bus height; Taiwan roads usually need about 4.6 m clear under a bridge plus the slab, which I recall but did not verify, so **guess**). The scene code uses 6.5 m for the deck top.
- Canopy: a thin curved roof about 2.8-3.2 m above the deck, so total walkway height about 3 m clear inside. **inferred** (photos 26, 27, 28; canopy sits about 1.7 body heights above the deck).
- Deck slab thickness: a slim box edge about 0.8-1.2 m deep (photo 30 underside). **inferred**
- Guard sides: tinted-glass panels about 1.1 m high with a steel handrail, then the canopy beam above. **inferred** (photos 26, 28).
- Columns: slender raked steel or round silver columns in pairs, about 12-20 m apart along a span, standing at the road edge or in the plaza. **inferred** (photo 27: pair spacing about 10-17 m against people about 1.7 m). Column diameter about 0.4-0.6 m. **guess**.
- Stairs: stairs run down beside the deck with curved stainless-steel balustrades, the same width as the deck or a little narrower, usually uncovered (photo 32). **inferred**
- Roof form: gently curved or flat with a pale underside; lit blue-white at night (photos 24, 30, 34). **inferred**
- Colours: pale grey/white canopy, light grey stone deck, blue-green tinted glass, silver columns. **inferred**
- Photo 29 (Songlong Road) is an older plain footbridge with concrete deck, stair ramps and steel railing, about 4 m wide. **inferred**. Do not use it for the Xinyi look.
