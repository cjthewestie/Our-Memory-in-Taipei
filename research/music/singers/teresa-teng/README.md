# 鄧麗君 Teresa Teng

鄧麗君 (Teresa Teng, born 29 January 1953 in 褒忠, Yunlin County; died 8 May 1995 in Chiang Mai, Thailand) is the singer most people across the Chinese-speaking world mean when they say "the old songs". She started on television and records in 1967, recorded for Yeu Jow (宇宙唱片, 1967–1971) and Life Records (1971/72–1976) in Taiwan and Hong Kong, then for Polydor Hong Kong from 1975 (distributed in Taiwan by 歌林 Kolin), and in Japan for Polydor from 1974 and Taurus Records from 1984. She sang in Mandarin, Japanese, Cantonese, Hokkien (Taiwanese) and English, and her Mandarin records reached mainland China in the late 1970s through smuggled cassettes, where they were denounced, banned and loved all at once. She is in this repo because `music.js` opens the 1980s list with her: `{ title: '望春風', artist: '鄧麗君', year: 1980, ... }`, the official-channel upload of her Hokkien recording of the 1933 Taiwanese standard, from the 福建名曲專輯 (Hokkien songs) album. It is the first sound a visitor hears when the scroll is in the 1985–1999 Ximending chapter, and the 大稻埕 1980s scene notes (`asset/dadaocheng-1980s.md`) already put 〈望春風〉 on the radio in 迪化街.

## Song by song

### 1. 千言萬語 (1973)

Written for 彩雲飛, the 1973 瓊瑤 film (English title *The Young Ones*), whose soundtrack Life Records issued with Teng, 尤雅 and 萬沙浪 sharing the songs. The soundtrack listing credits the music to 古月 (the pen name of 左宏元) and the lyrics to 爾英. It is a slow, lilting film ballad of the early-70s Taiwan studio type: strings and a light rhythm section under a vocal that sits mostly in her middle register, with the sweet, breathy, slightly girlish tone she had at twenty. The lyric is a lover's bewildered question about why the other has changed after so many words passed between them, the classic 瓊瑤 mood of devoted, mildly wounded love. Polydor Hong Kong had her re-record it for a 1977 greatest-hits album, which is the version most compilations carry. For the page it belongs to the memory of parents' generation rather than to the scroll's years: a 1970s song that would still have been on cassette in every Ximending record shop in the late 1980s. Chapter fit: 1985–1999, as an older track behind the 1980s list.

### 2. 海韻 (1974)

The title song of her 1974 album and the theme of the film 海韻. Per a Taiwanese media piece on 古月, 莊奴 reworked a poem by 徐志摩 into the lyric and 古月 set it; the song spread through the film. It is unusual among her early hits because it is a narrative, not a confession: an onlooker watches a young woman walking alone by the sea at dusk, calls to her to come home, and in the end she is gone, taken by the waves. The music follows that story with a rising, through-composed feel and big orchestral swells, closer to an art song than a pop single, and Teng sings it with restraint, almost as a narrator. It later became a school-choir and chorus-competition piece, which is how many Taiwanese of the 1980s and 1990s learned it. Its seriousness made it a template for the "literary" Mandopop of the next decade. Chapter fit: 1985–1999 as background memory; the poetic register suits a quiet, night-time moment of the Ximending chapter.

### 3. 空港 (1974)

Her second Japanese single, issued after a modest debut, and the song that made her a star in Japan. It won her the New Artist Award at the 16th Japan Record Awards in 1974 (sources are clear it was the New Artist Award, not the Best New Artist grand prize), and it was collected on her first Japanese studio album, 空港／雪化粧, released by Polydor on 21 October 1974. Sales figures differ by source, from over 700,000 in Japan to 750,000 across Asia. It is an enka-tinged kayōkyoku ballad: a woman leaves her lover at an airport rather than break up his life, a farewell played out in the departures hall. The arrangement is classic 1970s Japanese studio pop, strings and brass behind a steady beat, and Teng's Japanese is careful and clear, with the controlled vibrato that Japanese audiences prized. The song shows the second pillar of her career, Japan, which paid for and shaped everything after. Chapter fit: 1985–1999; Japanese pop was everywhere in late-80s Ximending, and this is one of its Taiwanese faces.

### 4. 月亮代表我的心 (1977)

Not her song first: 陳芬蘭 recorded it in 1973 (album 夢鄉, 麗歌唱片), and 劉冠霖 recorded it later the same year, with lyrics by 孫儀 and music by 翁清溪. Teng's version appeared on 島國之情歌第四集：香港之戀, released by Polydor and Kolin on 19 December 1977; the album was certified platinum by IFPI Hong Kong in 1979. One report notes the earlier version had trouble with Taiwan's censors over a kissing line, while her Hong Kong release did not. Musically it is a simple strophic ballad in a gentle triple-feel sway, small-combo and strings, sung softly and close to the microphone; the restraint is the whole point. The lyric answers "how much do you love me" with the moon, the line 月亮代表我的心 being the hook. Google's Doodle for her 65th birthday (29 January 2018) was built around this song. It is the most widely recognised Mandarin song in the world. Chapter fit: any; it was a karaoke standard in 1985–1999 KTVs in 西門町 and still is.

### 5. 何日君再來 (1978)

A 1937 Shanghai song, first recorded by 周璇 for the film 三星伴月, with music by 劉雪庵 and lyrics by 黃嘉謨. Teng's cover appeared on the Mandarin studio album *A Love Letter*, released on 21 December 1978 (one source dates her cover to 1979). The arrangement leans into the old Shanghai jazz-age style, a languid swing with spoken asides between verses, and she sings it lightly and a little playfully, as a toast at a parting dinner. The lyric is a farewell over wine: when will you come back? It became the most politically loaded song she sang. In Taiwan under martial law it was banned because 君 ("you, sir") sounded like 軍 ("army"), read as a hint at the return of the PLA or the Japanese army; on the mainland, the early-1980s campaign against "spiritual pollution" singled it out as decadent. Her songs spread on the mainland anyway. Chapter fit: 1985–1999, especially the late-80s years when martial law ended (1987) and banned songs came back.

### 6. 小城故事 (1979)

Written for 李行's 1978 film 小城故事 (*The Story of a Small Town*, starring 鍾鎮濤 and 林鳳嬌), which won Best Feature Film at the 16th Golden Horse Awards; 翁清溪 was nominated for the score. Music by 翁清溪, lyrics by 莊奴; Teng's record came out on 島國之情歌第六集：小城故事 on 23 March 1979, the track running about two and a half minutes. Short and bright, it is built on a bouncing, folk-flavoured melody with light percussion and a cheerful, almost hymn-like warmth, and Teng sings it with a smile in the voice. The lyric praises a small town full of good people and invites the listener to come and visit. It is a picture of the rural Taiwan that the 1980s city was leaving behind. Not a Golden Horse winner itself, as far as the sources show, but one of her most covered songs. Chapter fit: 1985–1999, as the countryside counter-image to Ximending; it would also suit the old-street 大稻埕 mood.

### 7. 甜蜜蜜 (1979)

Released as a single on 20 September 1979 and as the title track of the album 甜蜜蜜 (Polydor Hong Kong, 15 November 1979). The melody is the Indonesian folk song Dayung Sampan, which Teng had recorded in Indonesian while touring Southeast Asia; 莊奴 wrote the Mandarin words, by his own account in a few minutes, after being told the singer was Teng, whose round, sweet face suggested the title. It is a light, skipping folk-pop tune with a strong two-beat lilt, bright guitars and a cheerful arrangement, and Teng sings almost entirely in her clear head voice, the "sweet" sound that the mainland would later call her signature. The lyric is pure delight: a smile so sweet, like flowers in the spring breeze, and the feeling of having met this person before in a dream. She also recorded a Cantonese version for 勢不兩立 (1980). The 1996 Peter Chan film 甜蜜蜜 took its title and story from her songs. Chapter fit: 1985–1999.

### 8. 在水一方 (1980)

A cover of the theme of the 1975 瓊瑤 film 在水一方, first sung by 江蕾 and 高凌風, with lyrics by 瓊瑤 herself and music by 林家慶. Teng's version is the title track of her ninth Polydor Hong Kong studio album, released 12 February 1980, and it is the version most people know. The lyric adapts the imagery of the Book of Songs poem 蒹葭: the one I love stands on the other side of the water, and every path toward her is long and blocked. The arrangement is spacious and slow, strings and gentle flute-like colours, with a long-breathed melody that rises at each "on the other side of the water" phrase; Teng sings it almost without vibrato, letting the lines hang. It sits halfway between the film-song years and the classical-poetry album she would make three years later. Chapter fit: 1985–1999, a slow-scroll song; its water imagery also suits the 淡水河 side of 大稻埕.

### 9. 望春風 (1980) — the song on the page

The 1933 Taiwanese-language standard by 鄧雨賢 (music) and 李臨秋 (lyrics), one of the four 鄧雨賢 songs known together as 四月望雨, recorded in over sixty versions. Teng re-arranged and recorded it in 1980 for the 福建名曲專輯, a Hokkien album Polydor issued for Singapore, Malaysia and Hong Kong (LP 2427 343, cassette 3225 343); Discogs dates it 1980, a playlist note gives 20 January 1981. The same recordings were later reissued as 閩南語金曲集 (CD 1988), the album streaming services date to 1980. Its tracks include 賣肉粽, 三聲無奈, 安平追想曲 and 舊情綿綿. The lyric is a young woman alone on a spring night, catching sight of a handsome young man and hearing the door move in the wind, hoping it is him. Teng's version is gentle and polished, a soft pop arrangement around the old pentatonic tune, sung in careful Hokkien. Chapter fit: 1985–1999 as `music.js` has it; the radio in 迪化街 in the 1980s Dadaocheng notes plays it too.

### 10. 獨上西樓 (1983)

The opening track of 淡淡幽情 (1983, Polydor Hong Kong, Kolin in Taiwan), the first album Teng helped plan herself: twelve songs, each a setting of a classical ci poem. The words are 李煜's 相見歡, the deposed Southern Tang emperor alone in the west tower under a hook-shaped moon, and 劉家昌 wrote the music, with 盧東尼 arranging. It runs about two and three-quarter minutes. The arrangement blends Chinese instruments with a soft Western orchestra, and Teng sings low and slow, with a hushed, almost speaking delivery that is very different from her bright 1970s voice. The theme is the sorrow that "cuts but will not break", loss and exile. 淡淡幽情 was named album of the year at the 1983 Hong Kong music awards (per one source), and the project is remembered as the moment Mandopop showed classical poetry could be a hit. Chapter fit: 1985–1999 as a reflective track; it would suit a night view in the Ximending chapter.

### 11. 但願人長久 (1983)

Also from 淡淡幽情: 蘇軾's 水調歌頭, the Mid-Autumn poem, set by 梁弘志. The album credits list 蕭唯忱 as arranger; another source gives a different arranger, so that credit is uncertain. It is the album's most enduring track, and a review of the album notes it is almost the only one other singers dared to cover; 王菲 and 張學友 are among them. The melody is a broad, pentatonic ballad that builds gradually, and Teng phrases it with long legato lines over strings and plucked Chinese instruments, holding back until the final wish. The lyric looks at the full moon, asks heaven what year it is up there, accepts that people part and the moon waxes and wanes, and ends hoping that loved ones live long and share the same moonlight across a thousand miles. Many Taiwanese learned the poem through this record. Chapter fit: 1985–1999; every Mid-Autumn barbecue on a Taipei sidewalk, from then to now.

### 12. 漫步人生路 (1983)

The Cantonese version of 中島みゆき's ひとり上手 (Canyon Records, 1980), with new lyrics by 鄭國江; the recording is a PolyGram 1983 master arranged by 渡辺茂樹. One Hong Kong publication says Teng made only two Cantonese albums, 勢不兩立 (1980) and 漫步人生路 (1983), so this is almost certainly the title track of the second; that link is an inference. The arrangement is early-80s Hong Kong pop at its smoothest: a mid-tempo groove, electric piano and strings, a melody that climbs into a warm, open chorus. Teng's Cantonese is careful but natural, and her tone is fuller than on the Mandarin hits. The lyric, unlike the lonely Japanese original, is encouraging: walking life's road together, through rain and sun, without fear. It became a Hong Kong standard. For Taipei listeners in the 1980s it was one of the Cantonese songs that came across with Hong Kong films. Chapter fit: 1985–1999; the Ximending cinemas showed the Hong Kong films that made Cantonese pop familiar.

### 13. つぐない (1984)

Released on 21 January 1984 by Taurus Records with PolyGram, her comeback single in Japan after a gap in her Japanese career. Music by 三木たかし; one source credits the lyrics to 荒木とよひさ (with 林煌坤 linked to the Mandarin version, 償還). It peaked at No. 6 on the Oricon singles chart, placed 42nd for 1984 and 58th for 1985, and one source says it stayed in the top 10 for 41 weeks. It won the Grand Prix at the Japan Cable Awards, the first of three in a row. The song is a mature kayōkyoku ballad, slower and darker than her 1970s Japanese singles: a woman leaving the man she has hurt, telling herself the leaving is her atonement. The arrangement is lush strings and saxophone over a slow beat, and Teng's voice has dropped and thickened, with a smoky lower register. It opened the 三木たかし / 荒木とよひさ partnership that defined her last great run. Chapter fit: 1985–1999; 償還 was everywhere in Taipei in the mid-1980s.

### 14. 愛人 (1985)

The 1985 follow-up to つぐない, again for Taurus Records. The singles discography lists a peak of No. 10 on the Japanese chart and a digital Gold certification from the RIAJ; a fan account claiming a long run at No. 1 is not supported elsewhere. It brought her the second of three consecutive Japan Cable Awards Grand Prix. The credits widely cited are 荒木とよひさ (lyrics) and 三木たかし (music), but the sources consulted here do not confirm them. The song is a slow, sensual ballad: a woman who is the "other" lover asks only to stay close and be allowed to love, without demands. The arrangement continues the つぐない sound, string pads, soft saxophone and a slow pulse, and Teng's delivery is intimate, close-miked, with the lower, darker tone of her thirties. A Mandarin version was also recorded and is a familiar karaoke title in Taiwan (its credits are not verified here). Chapter fit: 1985–1999, the 1985 year itself, when the scroll first opens on Ximending.

### 15. 我只在乎你 (1987)

The Mandarin version of 時の流れに身をまかせ, the Japanese single released 21 February 1986, which peaked at No. 6 on Oricon, won her a third consecutive Japan Cable Awards Grand Prix, and was the song she sang on the 37th NHK Kōhaku Uta Gassen on 31 December 1986. Music by 三木たかし, Chinese lyrics by 慎芝; the title track of 我只在乎你, released by Polydor Hong Kong on 1 April 1987, her seventeenth studio album and her last original studio album in Mandarin. The lyric asks what she would be if she had never met him, and decides that she would give up everything else and let time flow by, if only she could be beside him; the hook is the title, 我只在乎你. The arrangement is a slow-building ballad with strings and a soft rhythm section that lifts into a sweeping chorus; her voice is warm, mature and steady. `research/music/README.md` calls it her late-career signature. Chapter fit: 1985–1999, squarely 1987, the year martial law ended.

## Patterns across the 15

- **Themes.** Love sung from the patient side: waiting (望春風, 在水一方), parting (空港, 何日君再來, つぐない), quiet devotion (月亮代表我的心, 我只在乎你). Almost no anger. The moon recurs as the measure of feeling (月亮代表我的心, 獨上西樓, 但願人長久).
- **Borrowed songs, owned versions.** Most of her best-known Mandarin hits were covers or adaptations: of earlier Taiwanese singers (月亮代表我的心, 在水一方), Shanghai (何日君再來), Indonesia (甜蜜蜜), 1930s Taiwan (望春風), Japan (漫步人生路, 我只在乎你), and classical poetry (淡淡幽情). Her versions became the reference ones.
- **Sound evolution.** Early-70s Taiwanese film-song orchestrations (千言萬語, 海韻) → late-70s Polydor Hong Kong light pop and folk-pop (甜蜜蜜, 小城故事) → the 1983 poetry album mixing Chinese instruments and orchestra → mid-80s Japanese studio ballads with saxophone and string pads (つぐない, 愛人, 我只在乎你).
- **Recurring collaborators.** 古月 (左宏元) in the Life Records years; 莊奴 (海韻, 小城故事, 甜蜜蜜) and 翁清溪 (月亮代表我的心, 小城故事) in the Polydor years; 三木たかし with 荒木とよひさ in Japan from 1984; 梁弘志 and 劉家昌 on 淡淡幽情; 瓊瑤 as lyricist and as the films that carried the songs.
- **Vocal changes.** A bright, light, slightly girlish head voice in the 1970s; by 1983 a deliberately hushed, lower delivery for the poetry; by 1984–87 a fuller, darker, more intimate tone with a lower centre, suited to the Japanese adult ballads.
- **Languages and markets.** Mandarin for Taiwan, Hong Kong and (unofficially) the mainland; Japanese for Japan; Cantonese for Hong Kong; Hokkien for Taiwan and the Singapore/Malaysia market. 望春風 sits in that last group.

## For the page

- **1980s list (`music.js` key `'1980s'`).** Keep 望春風 as the opener: it is the one Taiwanese-language song in the decade lists and matches the 大稻埕 1980s notes. If a Mandarin Teresa Teng song is wanted instead or as well, 我只在乎你 (1987) and 愛人 / 償還 (1985) fall inside the scroll's 1985–1989 years; 月亮代表我的心 is the most recognisable but dates from 1977.
- **1990s list.** None of the 15 is a 1990s release. Her death in May 1995 put her songs back on every Taipei radio station, but that is a memory of the decade, not a 1990s record; better to leave the 1990s list to 1990s singers.
- **2000s / 2010s lists.** Not a fit as releases. 但願人長久 lives on through 王菲's cover, which would belong to another singer's report.
- **Chorus candidates.** 望春風 is strophic, so there is no separate chorus; the strongest cue is the start of a later verse, after the instrumental break, and the current `start: 55` was set off that upload's own audio (see the `music.js` comments), not checked again here. 我只在乎你 has a true chorus that comes after two verses, where the arrangement opens up and the title line arrives; 月亮代表我的心 is also strophic, its hook being the title line at the end of each verse; 甜蜜蜜 has its title hook at the very start of the vocal. Any `start` value must be read off the specific upload, as `music.js` notes.
- **Licensing.** Nothing here is audio and nothing gets committed as audio. The page plays YouTube embeds only, and any real-audio use needs the sync and master licences set out in `research/music/README.md` (her Mandarin masters sit with Universal Music, ex-PolyGram; the Japanese-original songs add a JASRAC publisher).

## What I could not verify

- **Japanese credits for 空港 and 愛人** (山上路夫 / 猪俣公章; 荒木とよひさ / 三木たかし): widely cited, but no consulted source confirmed them. The 空港 single's exact release date was also not confirmed.
- **つぐない lyricist**: only one source names 荒木とよひさ; the 償還 Mandarin lyricist (林煌坤) and its release year (1984 or 1985) are not settled.
- **千言萬語 lyricist**: the soundtrack listing gives 爾英; other attributions circulate. Film and soundtrack are dated 1973; some sources say 1972.
- **海韻 label and date**: the 1974 album date is listed, but that Life Records issued it is inferred from her label years.
- **何日君再來 album**: English sources name it *A Love Letter* (21 December 1978); its Chinese title and track position were not confirmed, and one source dates her cover to 1979.
- **福建名曲專輯 date**: 1980 (Discogs) vs 20 January 1981 (playlist note); no Taiwan (Kolin) issue was confirmed. `music.js` uses 1980.
- **漫步人生路 album**: that the song is the title track of the 1983 Cantonese album is inferred from "only two Cantonese albums".
- **但願人長久 arranger**: 蕭唯忱 per the album article; another source names a different arranger.
- **淡淡幽情 award**: "album of the year, 1983 Hong Kong music awards" comes from one review only.
- **Debut year**: sources say 1966 or 1967; label start years (Yeu Jow 1967, Life 1971/72) come from one discography summary.
- **愛人 chart run**: peak No. 10 per the discography; a claimed 14-week No. 1 run is unsupported. Japan Cable Grand Prix for 1985 is supported only indirectly (the three-in-a-row claim).
- **No keys or tempos** are given anywhere in this file: none of the sources stated them.
