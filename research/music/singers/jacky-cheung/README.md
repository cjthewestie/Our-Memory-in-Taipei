# 張學友 Jacky Cheung

張學友 (born 10 July 1961, Hong Kong) is the Hong Kong singer known as 歌神, "God of Songs", and one of the 四大天王 of 1990s Cantopop. He won Hong Kong's first 18-district amateur singing contest in 1984 singing 大地恩情, was signed by PolyGram (寶麗金, the label that became part of Universal Music), and released his first album 《Smile》 in 1985. He recorded for PolyGram through the 1990s, stayed with the catalogue as it became Universal, released 2007's 《在你身邊 By Your Side》 through What's Music and Universal, and has spent the 2010s and 2020s mostly on stage: in February 2025 he passed 1,000 concerts across 10 tours. He is in this repo because the page's 1990s list opens with his Mandarin ballad, `{ title: '吻別', artist: '張學友', year: 1993 }` in `music.js`, played from the album master on the `張學友 Jacky Cheung (Topic)` channel and started at the first chorus. 《吻別》 was his fifth Mandarin album, and is reported as the second best-selling album of all time in Taiwan: the record that turned a Hong Kong Cantopop star into a Taipei household voice.

## Song by song

### 1. 遙遠的她 (1985)

The title song of his second album, 《遙遠的她 AMOUR》, released in 1985 roughly eight months after the platinum debut 《Smile》. One source traces the melody to a 1985 Japanese song by 谷村新司 (浪漫鐵道), whose original words had nothing to do with love, and credits the Cantonese lyric to 潘源良; neither credit could be checked against the sleeve. It follows the mid-80s Hong Kong pattern of a Japanese melody rewritten as a Cantonese love song: a slow ballad over synthesiser pads, electric piano and a soft backbeat, the voice placed close and clear above the band. What already stands out is the singing, long phrases held on an open, unforced tone rather than the heavy vibrato common at the time. The lyric is about missing someone who is far away and out of reach, the distance in the title being emotional as much as physical. In Taipei it was never a radio hit (Cantonese pop reached Taiwan mostly through tapes and Hong Kong films), so on the page it is at most a 1985–1999 Ximending-chapter footnote: the sound of the Hong Kong shelf in a 西門町 record shop, eight years before his Mandarin breakthrough.

### 2. 李香蘭 (1990)

A Cantonese cover of 玉置浩二's 行かないで, which he wrote in 1989 as the theme for a Japanese TV drama about the actress 李香蘭, which is where the Cantonese title comes from. The album it first appeared on, and the Cantonese lyricist, could not be confirmed (one song list shows 周禮茂 in an unlabelled column). The arrangement keeps the restraint of the Japanese original: a quiet piano-and-strings opening, verses sung almost at speaking volume, and a long climb to a chorus near the top of his range. The lyric reads as a reflection on a love bound up with a vanished era rather than a simple breakup, which suits the drama it came from. It is one of the recordings most often named when his reputation as an interpretive singer comes up, and it has been covered by other Hong Kong singers. For a Taipei listener it matters mostly through its Mandarin rewrite 秋意濃 (song 6), which is the version Taiwanese karaoke knows. It fits the 1985–1999 chapter, but as a pair with 秋意濃 the Mandarin version is the better choice for this page.

### 3. 每天愛你多一些 (1991)

His Cantonese cover of Southern All Stars' 真夏の果実, written by 桑田佳祐 as the theme of his 1990 film 稲村ジェーン. 張學友 recorded it in early 1991 for the album 《情不禁》, and it named his concert series that year (the live album is 《每天愛你多一些演唱會'91》). Sources report five straight weeks at number one on one Hong Kong chart and more than 30 weeks on radio charts, and it won a 1991 Jade Solid Gold award for its production (producer Michael Au). Musically it is a mid-tempo ballad with a gently rocking feel, guitar arpeggios and a lilting melody that sits comfortably in the middle of his voice, sung with warmth rather than drama. The Cantonese lyric is a plain promise of steadily growing devotion; the title is the hook. A Mandarin version was later recorded, but in Taiwan the same melody is better known as 剛澤斌's 妳在他鄉. In Taipei this one is a Cantonese karaoke standard rather than a radio memory, so it belongs to the 1985–1999 chapter as a Hong Kong echo.

### 4. 吻別 (1993)

The title song and lead single of his fifth Mandarin album, released by PolyGram on 5 March 1993, and the song the page already plays. Lyrics are by 何啟弘; 殷文琦 composed and arranged it. It won Song of the Year at Taiwan's Golden Melody Awards (sources agree on the award; one places it at the fifth ceremony), and the album is reported to have sold about 1.36 million copies in Taiwan and more than four million across Asia within three years. The arrangement is a slow-building power ballad: a soft keyboard-and-strings verse, a pre-chorus that lifts, and a chorus where drums, strings and a fuller band arrive and his voice opens up into a long sustained line. The lyric is a man replaying a final goodbye kiss and finding his world turned cold, with the chorus hook 「我和你吻別」 carrying the image. Michael Learns to Rock's 2004 Take Me to Your Heart is an English cover of this melody, a common source of confusion about which came first. It is the defining 1993 sound of Taipei's KTV boom and sits squarely in the page's 1985–1999 chapter, 1990s list.

### 5. 一路上有你 (1993)

A track on 《吻別》 (track 4 on the Hong Kong CD). The News Lens reports that 謝明訓 wrote the Mandarin lyric at PolyGram's request, over a melody by the Japanese composer 片山圭司 (泣けない君へのラブソング) that 張學友 had already sung in Cantonese in 1992 as 分手總要在雨天. The Mandarin version turns that rainy-day breakup song into its opposite: a song of gratitude for a partner who stayed with you all along the road. Musically it is a mid-tempo ballad with soft drums and strings, sung more gently than 吻別, the chorus built on the title phrase repeated at the end of each line. That warmth is why it became a fixture of Taiwanese weddings and anniversary dinners, the slow-dance number at a 喜宴 rather than the KTV heartbreak number. With 吻別 and 秋意濃 it made the 1993 album a run of hits rather than a one-song record. It fits the 1985–1999 chapter, 1990s list, and would be the warmer second Jacky Cheung option if the list ever wants contrast with 吻別.

### 6. 秋意濃 (1993)

Also on 《吻別》 (track 10 on the Hong Kong CD): 姚若龍's new Mandarin lyric for 玉置浩二's melody, the same tune 張學友 had sung in Cantonese as 李香蘭 (song 2). The Mandarin words move the song from history to season: autumn deepening, a love cooling, and memories surfacing as the weather turns. The arrangement keeps the slow, spacious build of the Cantonese version, piano and strings first, the band entering late, and his voice saved for the final choruses, where he reaches the top of the melody at full voice. It is one of the clearest examples of the 1990s Taiwan practice of taking a Hong Kong (and ultimately Japanese) hit and giving it a fresh Mandarin lyric from a Taipei writer, which is exactly how much of the 吻別 album was assembled. In Taipei it is an autumn KTV perennial and a standard showcase piece for confident singers, because the last chorus is so exposed. It belongs in the 1985–1999 chapter; for the page it is the most "Taipei-made" of the three 吻別 album songs, since both the lyricist and the market were Taiwanese.

### 7. 祝福 (1993)

The title song of 《祝福》, released about nine months after 《吻別》 in 1993. Lyrics are credited to 丁曉雯 and music to 郭子 (the songwriter also written as 郭蘅祈; one article uses each name). It won Song of the Year at the sixth Golden Melody Awards, his second in a row by most accounts. Where 吻別 is a private heartbreak, 祝福 is a public farewell: a slow ballad meant to be sung together, with a broad, hymn-like melody, a gradually swelling arrangement, and a chorus that invites the audience to join. The lyric wishes a departing friend well and promises they will not be forgotten. That made it a Taiwanese ceremony song, played at graduations, farewells for colleagues and send-offs at the airport, and it is still a closing number at karaoke nights. The album also carried heartbreak ballads such as 對你的愛越深就越來越心痛 and 最後一封信, confirming the 吻別 formula. 祝福 fits the 1985–1999 chapter, 1990s list, and is the strongest candidate besides 吻別 for that list: it is the song Taipei sings in groups.

### 8. 這個冬天不太冷 (1994)

A Cantonese song with lyrics by 陳少琪 and music by 張學友 himself, from what one article calls his fifteenth Cantonese album (1994; the album title in the source was cut off and is not given here). It is included to show a side of him the Taipei hits hide: he writes melodies. The song is a gentle winter ballad, mid-tempo, built on acoustic guitar and soft keyboards, with an easy, conversational melody that suggests a singer writing for his own natural range rather than for display. The lyric, as summarised by sources, turns a cold season into a warm one through companionship, a mood close to 一路上有你. It was a Hong Kong hit, not a Taiwanese one, and Taipei listeners will know it far less well than the Mandarin ballads, which is why it is the only 1994 entry and one of only five Cantonese songs in this list. It would belong to the 1985–1999 chapter, but it is not a candidate for the page's 1990s list; it is here for the career picture and as evidence for the songwriter credit he later took on albums such as 《想和你去吹吹風》.

### 9. 我等到花兒也謝了 (by 1995)

A Mandarin ballad that appears as track 3 on the 1995 compilation 《真愛新曲+精選》 and is in the setlist of the live album from his 1995 Taiwan concerts, 《情緣十載 '95友學友台灣演唱會》. Its first studio album and its lyricist could not be confirmed. A report on 一千個傷心的理由 says 李偲菘 wrote that song after writing this one, which makes 李偲菘 the likely composer, but that is an inference from an article about a different song. The title states the whole lyric: waiting for a lover so long that the flowers have wilted. It is a slow, classic 90s Mandarin ballad: a sparse opening, a long verse, and a chorus that rises to a held high note, the style 李偲菘 would refine in 一千個傷心的理由. In Taipei it is a karaoke staple, and its place in a Taiwan concert setlist in 1995 shows it was already a crowd song there. It fits the 1985–1999 chapter; because its year and album are not pinned down, it should not go into `music.js` until a sleeve or KKBOX credit is checked.

### 10. 一千個傷心的理由 (1995)

A new song on the 1995 Mandarin compilation 《真愛新曲+精選》, with lyrics by 邢增華 and music by the Singaporean songwriter 李偲菘. It is one of his biggest Mandarin hits after 吻別 and, for many Taiwanese listeners, the definitive mid-90s heartbreak song. The structure is a long, slow verse that lists reasons and memories, then a chorus that climbs to his strongest high register, backed by strings and a full band; the effect depends on his control, staying soft through the verse and only opening up at the hook. The lyric is a lover who can find a thousand reasons to grieve but none to stop loving, and the title is the hook. Sources treat it as the high point of the 李偲菘 collaboration (song 9 being the earlier piece). For Taipei it is the song of 1995–1996 KTV rooms and cassette players in taxis, and it fits the 1985–1999 chapter. It is the best alternative to 吻別 for the 1990s list if the page ever wants a different Jacky Cheung song, since the chorus is its whole point.

### 11. 情書 (1996)

From 《忘記你我做不到》, his ninth Mandarin album, released in 1996; lyrics by 姚若龍 (who also wrote 秋意濃) and music by 戚小戀. It is a soft ballad built around the image of a handwritten love letter: rereading old letters, the feelings preserved in them, and the gap between what was written and what happened. Musically it is gentler than the 1993–1995 power ballads: acoustic guitar and piano carry the verse, strings fill in rather than surge, and he sings much of it at half voice, which makes the final chorus lift more striking. The album marks the point where his Mandarin records moved from the big-ballad formula toward more intimate arrangements, a turn that 《想和你去吹吹風》 completed the following year. In Taipei 情書 is remembered as a late-90s radio and karaoke ballad, and its subject now carries extra nostalgia, the handwritten letter itself having become a memory. It fits the 1985–1999 chapter; for the page it is a softer, less obvious 1990s pick that still sounds unmistakably like him.

### 12. 愛是永恆 (1997)

The love theme of 雪狼湖 (Snow.Wolf.Lake), the musical 張學友 helped plan and direct and in which he starred; it opened at the Hong Kong Coliseum on 28 March 1997 and ran for 42 performances, and is described as Hong Kong's first modern musical. The song's individual credits could not be confirmed; the production's music and lyrics are credited to a team that includes Dick Lee and 張學友 among others. The original is in Cantonese; a Mandarin production followed, and its cast album 《雪狼湖創意音樂劇》 earned him a 2006 Golden Melody nomination for best Mandarin male singer. As a theatre song it is built differently from his pop ballads: a long melodic arc written for a character, orchestral rather than band-led, sung with more open, projected tone and a long sustained climax. The lyric is a vow that love outlasts loss, in keeping with the musical's tragic romance. In Taipei it is known mainly to fans and through the Mandarin version, so it sits at the edge of the 1985–1999 chapter; it belongs here because it is his landmark outside pop.

### 13. 想和你去吹吹風 (1997)

The title song of his 1997 Mandarin album 《想和你去吹吹風》, which won him the Golden Melody best male singer award (the 歌王 title) at the ninth ceremony in 1998, one of the three Golden Melody awards he has won. Song credits could not be verified here. It is a break from the 吻別 template: instead of a dramatic power ballad, it is a relaxed, mid-tempo song with a light, almost swaying groove, warm acoustic and electric guitars, and a vocal sung close and casual, closer to a conversation than a performance. The lyric is simple and grown-up: wanting nothing more than to go out with the person you love and feel the wind, love as an ordinary afternoon rather than a tragedy. That restraint is what the Golden Melody jury rewarded, and it shows his voice in its most natural register. In Taipei it is the late-90s song for a scooter ride along the river or a drive to 淡水, and it fits the 1985–1999 chapter; it is the best 1990s pick here if the page wants a mood other than heartbreak.

### 14. 心如刀割 (1999)

From 《走過1999》, his twelfth Mandarin album, released at the end of the decade (the album's 1999 date is inferred from its title; uDiscover Music calls the album a Mandopop masterpiece; the song's credits were not found). It returns to the big-ballad style after the lighter 1997 album: a slow, string-heavy arrangement, a long, intense chorus, and a vocal that pushes into the top of his range with more grit than in his early-90s recordings. The lyric is a breakup told as physical pain, the title being the hook. The album is often treated as a late peak of his Mandarin work. In Taipei it is a karaoke song for confident singers rather than a sing-along, and its turn-of-the-millennium date puts it right on the border of the page's chapters: released in the last year of the 1985–1999 Ximending chapter, but heard through the early 2000s. For `music.js` it fits the 1990s list, but it would not add much beside 吻別; it is in this list for the career arc.

### 15. 用餘生去愛 (2014)

The lead single of 《醒著做夢》 (Wake Up Dreaming), his 2014 Mandarin album marking 30 years since his debut and his first Mandarin album in seven years. Many listeners took the single's title to be the album's. At the 26th Golden Melody Awards (2015) the song was nominated for Song of the Year, he was nominated for best male singer, and the album for best Mandarin album; the results were not confirmed here. A TVBS report from November 2014 said the song was inspired by a true story. Song credits could not be verified. It is an adult ballad in his late style: a quieter, more measured arrangement, a lower centre of gravity in the melody, and a voice that is darker and grainier than in 1993 but more controlled, with the climax reached through intensity rather than height. The lyric is a promise to spend the rest of a life loving one person, a mature answer to the early heartbreak songs. It fits the 2000–2019 Dadaocheng chapter and is the only song here that could go in the 2010s list, as a reminder that the 1993 voice is still singing.

## Patterns across the 15

- **Themes.** Parting dominates: a goodbye kiss (吻別), a send-off (祝福), waiting in vain (我等到花兒也謝了), a thousand reasons to grieve (一千個傷心的理由), a heart cut (心如刀割). The counterweight songs are about staying: 一路上有你, 每天愛你多一些, 想和你去吹吹風, 用餘生去愛. Over 30 years the emphasis moves from losing someone to keeping someone.
- **Japanese melodies, Taiwanese lyrics.** Four of the 15 rest on Japanese compositions (遙遠的她 if the attribution holds, 李香蘭/秋意濃 by 玉置浩二, 每天愛你多一些 by 桑田佳祐, 一路上有你 by 片山圭司). The 1993 breakthrough was built by giving Hong Kong and Japanese melodies new Mandarin lyrics from Taipei writers.
- **Recurring collaborators.** 姚若龍 (秋意濃, 情書) and 李偲菘 (一千個傷心的理由, probably 我等到花兒也謝了) are the two names that recur in the Mandarin catalogue; 殷文琦 and 何啟弘 made 吻別; 郭子 and 丁曉雯 made 祝福; 謝明訓 wrote for him at PolyGram's request. 張學友 also composes (這個冬天不太冷).
- **Sound evolution.** Mid-80s synth-pad ballads → early-90s power ballads with full strings and a big final chorus (1993–1995) → lighter, more acoustic, conversational records (情書, 想和你去吹吹風) → a late-90s return to the big ballad (心如刀割) → theatre writing (雪狼湖) → restrained adult ballads (用餘生去愛).
- **Vocal changes.** The 1985 voice is bright and light; by 1990–1993 it has the range and long breath that made the power ballads work; by 1997 he is choosing understatement; by 2014 it is darker and lower, with less top but more weight. Through all of it the trademark is diction and line, every word clear.
- **Taiwan recognition.** Three Golden Melody awards: Song of the Year for 吻別 and 祝福, and best male singer for 《想和你去吹吹風》.

## For the page

- **1990s list in `music.js`.** 吻別 already holds the slot and is the right choice. If a second Jacky Cheung song is ever wanted, 祝福 (the song Taipei sings together) or 一千個傷心的理由 (the mid-90s heartbreak ballad) are the strongest; 想和你去吹吹風 is the pick for a lighter mood. Keep one Jacky Cheung song per list so the decade is not one voice.
- **2010s list.** 用餘生去愛 (2014) is the only candidate here; it is a respectable choice but not a Taipei-wide memory the way 後來的我們 is, so it is a reserve, not a replacement.
- **Chorus candidates.** In 祝福 the chorus is the broad, hymn-like refrain after a fairly short first verse; in 一千個傷心的理由 the chorus is where the melody climbs after a long first verse, so a start near the first chorus skips a quiet minute; in 想和你去吹吹風 the title line opens the chorus. Any start time must be read off the specific upload, the way the comment above `YT_VIDEOS` describes; none are given here.
- **Licensing.** Everything above is PolyGram catalogue, now Universal Music, so the master owner is a major and the cost profile in `research/music/README.md` applies (a fee and a month or more). The page plays YouTube embeds and nothing is committed as audio; that rule stands for every song in this file.
