# 周杰倫 Jay Chou

周杰倫 Jay Chou is the Taiwanese singer-songwriter, producer and director who redrew Mandopop after his debut album 《Jay》 came out on 6 November 2000 on 阿爾發音樂 Alfa Music, the label run by 吳宗憲 that had signed him first as a songwriter. Active from 2000 to now, he moved from Alfa's early releases (several distributed through Sony Music Taiwan, which is the label named on 《葉惠美》) to his own company 杰威爾音樂 JVR Music, which put out his later singles and albums. He writes the music for nearly everything he sings; most of his lyrics come from 方文山 Vincent Fang, with 徐若瑄 Vivian Hsu, 黃俊郎 and Jay himself as the other main lyricists. He is in this repo because the page's music player plays one of his songs for the 2000s: the `music.js` entry is `title: '七里香', artist: '周杰倫', year: 2004` (official upload on the 周杰倫 Jay Chou channel, `start: 80`), sitting in the `'2000s'` list that scores chapter 2, the 2000–2019 Dadaocheng stretch of the scroll. `research/music/README.md` lists the same song as "Peak Jay Chou; the sound of the decade's summers".

## Song by song

### 1. 可愛女人 (2000)

The lead single of 《Jay》, released in November 2000 when Jay Chou was an unknown Alfa Music staff songwriter whose songs other singers had turned down. 徐若瑄 wrote the lyric, her first lyric for another artist, and also played the girl in the music video, which gave the newcomer a famous face to sit next to. Musically it is laid-back R&B on a programmed groove, built on short, repeated phrases; Jay's vocal is soft, half-mumbled and close to the microphone, the delivery that critics at first mocked as unclear diction and fans soon imitated. The lyric is a shy, slightly comic crush: a boy who cannot get the words out, circling the girl he likes. The album won Best Pop Vocal Album at the 12th Golden Melody Awards, beating established stars. For Taipei, this is the sound of the turn of the millennium: Walkmans and early MP3 players, Ximending record shops putting a new name in the window. It would fit the very start of the 2000–2019 chapter, the hinge between the Ximending years and Dadaocheng.

### 2. 星晴 (2000)

Also from 《Jay》, and described in Taiwanese retrospectives as the simplest and sweetest small piece on the album. It is a short, gentle love song in a light pop-R&B frame, mostly acoustic guitar and soft keyboards with a lilting, almost nursery-rhyme melody that sits in an easy range, which is why it became a campus and guitar-club staple. The lyric pictures two people under a starry night sky, a wish-on-a-star kind of first love; the title itself is a pun, mixing 星 (star) into the word for clear weather. No chart or award claims are attached to it in the sources consulted; its place on this list is as a long-running fan favourite and singalong. The Taipei memory is of school trips, Yangmingshan night views and rooftop stargazing before the city's light washed out the sky. Chapter fit: 2000–2019, early in the chapter. Its credits are less well sourced than the rest of the list; see the last section.

### 3. 簡單愛 (2001)

From 《范特西》, released in September 2001, the album that took five awards at the 13th Golden Melody Awards, including Best Pop Vocal Album. 徐若瑄 again wrote the lyric, with music by Jay Chou; the album notes list the song as R&B. The arrangement is bright and loose: acoustic guitar, a relaxed groove and a melody that moves in small steps, with Jay singing it lightly and almost conversationally. The lyric is about the most uncomplicated kind of love: riding a bike, holding hands, not needing anything grand, and the chorus wish to just love someone simply. It became a karaoke and school-graduation standard, the song people of a certain age name when they say Jay Chou was their youth. The Taipei image is a scooter or bicycle ride along the Tamsui riverside or the old Dadaocheng wharf in early summer. Chapter fit: 2000–2019, early.

### 4. 雙截棍 (2001)

The other face of 《范特西》. 方文山 wrote the lyric and Jay the music; it is a fast rap-rock track built on heavy guitar riffs, martial-arts sound effects and a breathless, rhythm-first vocal in which Jay raps more than he sings. The lyric is a tribute to kung-fu cinema and Bruce Lee, with nunchaku, temples and fighting stances as images, played half seriously and half as a joke. Taiwanese press later recalled that the song travelled as far as Europe, where it briefly topped request charts on a local MTV channel. It is also the song that fixed the 方文山–周杰倫 partnership in the public mind; 方文山 won Best Lyricist at the same awards for another song on the album, 威廉古堡. For the page it carries the energy of 2001 Ximending: arcades, kung-fu film posters and teenagers trying the moves. Chapter fit: 2000–2019, though it would also suit the tail of the Ximending years as a bridge.

### 5. 晴天 (2003)

A song from 《葉惠美》, released by Sony Music Taiwan on 31 July 2003 and named after Jay Chou's mother. 晴天 is a band ballad: it opens on an acoustic guitar figure that generations of beginners have learned as their first song, then builds with drums, bass and electric guitar into a long, emotional ending with a guitar solo. The vocal moves from quiet storytelling in the verses to a fuller, more open chorus. The lyric looks back at a school-days love that was never quite spoken, with rain and sunshine as the two moods of memory. Taiwanese news coverage regularly calls it one of his classic love songs, and it is among the most requested Jay Chou songs in KTV and on streaming services, though no single sourced chart figure is cited here. For Taipei, it belongs to afternoon thunderstorms, school corridors and the sudden blue sky after the rain. Chapter fit: 2000–2019. Credits: see the last section.

### 6. 東風破 (2003)

Also on 《葉惠美》, and the song credited with starting the 方文山–周杰倫 "中國風" (Chinese style) line: classical Chinese imagery in the words, pentatonic melody and Chinese instrument colours over a modern R&B ballad backing. 方文山 wrote the lyric; the song helped earn Jay Best Composer at the Chinese Music Media Awards. The arrangement layers Chinese plucked and bowed instrument sounds over strings and a slow beat, and Jay sings in a restrained, legato style rather than his usual clipped delivery. The lyric is a farewell scene drawn from old poetry: a lonely lamp, an old tune, the east wind and a lover gone. It made classical vocabulary fashionable in pop and started a run of similar songs (菊花台, 青花瓷 and later ones). The Taipei link is the old city: temple eaves, tea houses and the 大稻埕 shophouses that the page's second chapter is built around. Chapter fit: 2000–2019, a strong match for the Dadaocheng scene.

### 7. 七里香 (2004)

The song already on the page. The title track of Jay Chou's fifth studio album, released on 3 August 2004 on Alfa Music; the single went to Taiwanese radio on 21 July 2004. 方文山 wrote the lyric and Jay the music. It is a mid-tempo R&B love song led by acoustic guitar and strings, with a relaxed, swaying groove and a vocal that is gentle in the verses and warmer, more sustained in the chorus. The lyric is a summer memory of a girl, with the scent of the orange-jessamine flower (七里香), sparrows outside the window and a night of rain, the hook beginning 「雨下整夜」. It was number one on Hit FM's 2004 year-end singles chart, won a HITO Radio Music Award as one of the ten songs of the year and the listeners' favourite, and the album received six Golden Melody nominations. For Taipei, it is the summer of 2004: school holidays, cicadas and the sound coming out of every convenience store. Chapter fit: 2000–2019, as `music.js` already has it.

### 8. 夜曲 (2005)

The lead single of 《十一月的蕭邦》, released on 1 November 2005, an album framed around Chopin. 夜曲 means "nocturne", and the song mixes a classical, minor-key piano mood with Jay's R&B rap verses and a sung, sweeping chorus, with strings adding a dark, cinematic colour. The lyric, usually credited to 方文山, is a lament for a lost lover, with gothic images of night, roses, crows and a funeral, delivered half as poetry and half as rap. It was one of the most-played Mandarin songs of late 2005 in Taiwan, though this report has no sourced chart figure for it. The Taipei memory is of winter nights and the city's piano-practice culture, every child in the city trying to play the intro. Chapter fit: 2000–2019. Credits and chart details: see the last section.

### 9. 菊花台 (2006)

The theme song for 張藝謀's film 《滿城盡帶黃金甲》, in which Jay Chou also acted alongside 周潤發 and 鞏俐, and collected on the album 《依然范特西》. Lyric by 方文山, music by Jay, arrangement by 鍾興民. It is 中國風 at its most orchestral: a slow ballad carried by strings, with a melody built on Chinese pentatonic turns, and a vocal that stays soft and drawn out throughout. The lyric draws on palace tragedy, a chrysanthemum terrace, broken flowers and a lover who will not return, matching the film's fall of a royal family. It became one of the best-known Chinese film songs of the decade and a common choice in singing competitions. For Taipei, it is a cinema song: queues at the Ximending theatres in late 2006 for a mainland blockbuster. Chapter fit: 2000–2019, as a counterpart to 東風破.

### 10. 不能說的秘密 (2007)

The title song of the 2007 film Jay Chou wrote, directed and starred in opposite 桂綸鎂, set in a school in Tamsui. Lyric by 方文山, music by Jay, arrangement by 林邁可. It is a piano-led ballad with light percussion and strings; the vocal is understated, close and tender. The lyric is about a love that has to stay secret and a feeling that slips away while you hesitate, which is also the film's plot in miniature. The film's piano duel scene and its antique piano became pop-culture objects in their own right: Taiwan News reported the piano going on display at the Taipei Music Center. For this repo, it is one of the few songs on the list with a direct place link to greater Taipei, the riverside school and streets of Tamsui at the end of the MRT line. Chapter fit: 2000–2019; a good alternative to 七里香 for the 2000s list if a Taipei-located song is wanted.

### 11. 青花瓷 (2007)

From 《我很忙》 (2007). Lyric by 方文山, music by Jay Chou. At the 19th Golden Melody Awards, held at Taipei Arena on 5 July 2008, it won Song of the Year, Best Composer and Best Lyricist, the most complete recognition any song on this list received. It is the most refined of the 中國風 songs: a slow, elegant ballad with Chinese plucked-string colours, strings and a light beat, sung with a gentle, careful legato and very clear diction, a change from the mumbled early style. The lyric compares a longed-for woman to the blue-and-white porcelain of the Ming dynasty, with brush painting, misty rain and waiting as recurring images; the best-known hook starts 「天青色等煙雨」. It entered school textbooks and exam questions in the Chinese-speaking world as an example of modern lyric writing. The Taipei link is the National Palace Museum's porcelain galleries. Chapter fit: 2000–2019.

### 12. 稻香 (2008)

The lead single of 《魔杰座》, released on 15 October 2008, with both lyric and music by Jay Chou. It won Song of the Year at the 20th Golden Melody Awards in 2009, and was also nominated for lyric and composition. It is a cheerful, mid-tempo pop song with acoustic guitar and a relaxed, rap-inflected verse, opening a mood of countryside calm. Jay said in a 2013 interview that it was written in response to the 2008 Wenchuan earthquake: the lyric tells listeners not to give up after failure and to go home to simple things, rice fields, fireflies and childhood. The song became one of his most quoted comfort songs, played at graduations and in hard times. For Taipei, it is the city looking back at the countryside its residents came from, the train south at Lunar New Year. Chapter fit: 2000–2019, and with its back-to-roots message it is a good close for the 2000s list.

### 13. 告白氣球 (2016)

From 《周杰倫的床邊故事》 (2016). Lyric by 方文山, music by Jay. It is a light, breezy love song with a bouncing, almost French-café feel, acoustic textures and a smiling vocal, short and easy to sing. The lyric uses a balloon as a playful image for asking someone out, and encourages listeners not to be afraid to confess their feelings. According to Taiwanese press, it was the fastest Chinese pop video of its time to pass 100 million YouTube views and the most-watched Chinese song on YouTube for three years; CNA reported that it had been on charts for 362 consecutive days by June 2017, the longest run among that year's Golden Melody Song of the Year nominees. For Taipei, it is the Instagram era: date spots along the Xinyi district, phone videos and wedding-proposal flash mobs. Chapter fit: 2000–2019, late in the chapter; it is the obvious candidate for the `'2010s'` list.

### 14. 說好不哭 (2019)

A digital single released at 11 p.m. on 16 September 2019 on JVR Music, music by Jay, lyric by 方文山, sung as a duet with 阿信 of 五月天 Mayday. It is a slow piano-and-strings ballad in the classic Jay Chou mould, with the two voices trading lines, 阿信's brighter tone set against Jay's softer one. The lyric is about two people who promised each other not to cry when they parted, and about keeping that promise. On release, QQ Music reported more than 15 million RMB in digital sales by the next morning, later reported as over 22 million, the highest-grossing digital single in the platform's history, and the launch briefly overloaded its servers. Reviews were mixed (one Douban score cited was 6.1), but the commercial event was huge. For Taipei, it joins the two biggest names of Taiwanese pop on one track, in a year when both were stadium fixtures. Chapter fit: the last year of 2000–2019, or the doorway to 2020–.

### 15. 最偉大的作品 (2022)

The title track of Jay Chou's fifteenth album, released on 15 July 2022, six years after his previous full album. The lyric is by 黃俊郎, not 方文山; he explained that most of the words point to a painting, an essay, a poem or a historical reference. Music is by Jay. The song is built on piano with a waltz-like sway and orchestral colour, and Jay's vocal is lighter and more reflective, with some rapped lines. The lyric imagines travelling back to early 20th-century Paris and meeting artists and their works; the music video drew attention for references such as Magritte's man in a black hat, and passed ten million YouTube views in two days. For Taipei, it is a post-pandemic song: a city that could not travel looking at the world through art. Chapter fit: 2020–, the only song on the list that belongs to the Taipei 101 chapter.

## Patterns across the 15

- **Themes.** First love and its memory (可愛女人, 星晴, 簡單愛, 晴天, 七里香, 告白氣球) is the core. Next to it run classical Chinese imagery (東風破, 菊花台, 青花瓷), martial-arts and film culture (雙截棍, 菊花台, 不能說的秘密), and encouragement or comfort (稻香, 說好不哭). Late songs turn to art and time (最偉大的作品).
- **Sound evolution.** 2000–2001: R&B, hip-hop and rap-rock, programmed beats, a deliberately blurred vocal. 2003–2007: the 中國風 ballads with pentatonic melodies and Chinese instrument colours over Western strings and beats, alongside acoustic guitar ballads. 2005 adds classical piano (夜曲, then the film). From 2016: lighter, acoustic, more "timeless" pop with piano and orchestra.
- **Collaborators.** 方文山 wrote the lyrics for 10 of the 15 (and is usually credited for 夜曲). 徐若瑄 wrote the two earliest love songs; 黃俊郎 the 2022 title track; Jay himself 稻香 and, by common credit, 星晴 and 晴天. Arrangers named in sources: 鍾興民 (菊花台), 林邁可 (不能說的秘密). Duet partner: 阿信 of 五月天.
- **Vocal changes.** The early mumbled, rhythm-first delivery (可愛女人, 雙截棍) gives way to clearer diction and long legato lines in the 中國風 ballads (青花瓷 is the clearest example), then to a softer, lighter, more reflective tone in the 2016–2022 songs.
- **Awards.** The big Golden Melody results cluster in two places: the albums 《Jay》 and 《范特西》 (2001–2002), and the songs 青花瓷 and 稻香 (2008–2009).

## For the page

- **2000s list (`'2000s'` in `music.js`):** 七里香 stays. Good second and third entries from Jay's own catalogue would be 簡單愛 (2001) or 晴天 (2003) for recognition, or 不能說的秘密 (2007) for its Tamsui setting. 東風破 or 青花瓷 suit the Dadaocheng scene visually. Note that `research/music/README.md` already lists other artists for the 2000s; one Jay Chou song per decade list is probably enough.
- **2010s list (`'2010s'`):** 告白氣球 (2016) is the strongest candidate; 說好不哭 (2019) pairs Jay with 五月天, whose 後來的我們 already sits in that list, so pick one or the other to avoid two Mayday voices.
- **2020– (Taipei 101 chapter):** `music.js` has no 2020s list today. If one is added, 最偉大的作品 (2022) is the only song here that fits.
- **Chorus candidates (described, not timed):** 七里香's chorus arrives after two verses, where the vocal opens up over fuller strings (the current `start: 80` already targets it). 晴天's chorus arrives after a long guitar-led verse; its strongest moment is the final chorus before the guitar solo. 青花瓷 and 東風破 put the title hook at the start of the chorus. 告白氣球 reaches its chorus quickly, within the first minute or so. 稻香's chorus comes after the rap-inflected verse. Any `start` value must be read off the specific upload, as the comment in `music.js` explains.
- **Licensing:** nothing here may be committed as audio. All of these masters and compositions belong to Alfa Music, JVR Music or Sony and the named lyricists' publishers; the steps and costs are in `research/music/README.md` ("What licensing a real song takes"). The YouTube embed plays the rights holder's own upload and stores nothing in the repo.
